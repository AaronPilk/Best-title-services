Common questions

Title insurance, answered

Practically all of them. The stakes climb with commercial and investment deals, where prices are higher and purchase agreements are more complicated, so a defect costs considerably more to resolve after the fact.

It protects the investor when a problem surfaces on the title after closing. Those problems usually trace back to human error — a misfiled document, a clerical mistake, occasionally fraud.

Without a policy, the investor absorbs the loss. With one, the underwriter defends the claim.

There are two. The lender’s policy protects the lender’s stake and is required when there is financing. The owner’s policy protects you. A buyer using a lender is typically responsible for both.

The owner’s policy stays in force as long as you hold an interest in the property — no renewal, no expiration.
